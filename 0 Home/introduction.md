# Best practice for this kind of vault

This vault is designed with two different but connected goals:

1. To act as a knowledge base for concepts and ideas you learn while studying wireless communication and 5G PHY.
2. To act as a book-like learning path, where you can follow a linear sequence of topics and build understanding step by step.

## Core principle

Do not treat every note as equal. In a technical vault, notes usually fall into three different categories:

- Concept notes: explain a topic, a definition, a model, or an idea.
- Learning-path notes: represent a chapter or a milestone in the study order.
- Attachment notes: diagrams, screenshots, equations, or figures that support a concept.

If all notes are thrown into one flat list, the vault becomes hard to navigate and hard to review later.

## Recommended workflow

### 1. Use concept notes for exploration

A concept note should answer one question clearly and often be short, atomic, and reusable.

Examples:
- OFDM
- Delay spread
- Doppler effect
- Channel state information
- Beamforming
- Resource grid

These notes can be linked from many places and are useful even when you are not following a strict study order.

### 2. Use learning-path notes for a linear study sequence

The learning path should be a deliberate order of chapters or topics that help you move from basic to advanced topics.

Examples:
- Frame structure and numerology
- PSS/SSS
- PBCH and MIB
- PDCCH
- PDSCH
- PRACH
- PUSCH
- PUCCH
- SRS

These notes should not try to be the same as concept notes. They are chapter checkpoints, and they should link to related concept notes.

### 3. Keep attachments separate

Screenshots, equations, and external figures are useful, but they become messy if they are mixed with conceptual writing.

Use the attachment folder for:
- diagrams from books or papers
- reference screenshots
- handwritten notes converted to images
- plots and figures

Each attachment should be connected to the relevant concept note or chapter note with a clear description.

### 4. Make notes linkable

The strongest Obsidian vault is not the one with the most folders. It is the one where the notes connect well.

A good note usually includes:
- a short summary
- definitions
- formulas when needed
- links to prerequisites
- links to related topics
- a note about what is still unclear

This makes recall and revision much easier.

## How to organize this vault

Use a simple structure:

- Foundations: mathematics, signals, digital signal processing
- Wireless Communication: channel, fading, modulation, MIMO, beamforming, OFDM, CSI
- 5G NR PHY Learning Path: ordered chapters for 5G study
- MOCs and Indexes: maps of content and learning indexes
- Attachments: figures and supporting materials
- Temporary thoughts: rough notes, quick ideas, and scratch writing

## Important rule

The vault should support both of these ways of learning:

- free exploration of ideas
- structured progression through 5G concepts

If one dominates the other, the vault becomes less useful. The goal is not perfection; the goal is clarity.

## Final reminder

When you add a new note, ask yourself:

- Is this a concept note, a chapter note, or an attachment?
- Does it link to related notes?
- Is this note part of the learning path or part of the reference map?

If you keep those questions in mind, the vault stays useful even as it grows.
